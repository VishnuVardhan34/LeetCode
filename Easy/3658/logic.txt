Problem: GCD of Odd and Even Sums

Logic:
1. Compute the sum of the first `n` odd numbers using the mathematical formula:
      sumOdd = n²
2. Compute the sum of the first `n` even numbers using the mathematical formula:
      sumEven = n × (n + 1)
3. Find the Greatest Common Divisor (GCD) of `sumOdd` and `sumEven` using the Euclidean Algorithm:
   - While the second number is not zero:
     - Store the second number in a temporary variable.
     - Replace the second number with the remainder of the division (`a % b`).
     - Replace the first number with the temporary value.
   - When the second number becomes zero, the first number is the GCD.
4. Return the computed GCD.

Why it works:
- The sum of the first `n` odd numbers is always `n²`.
- The sum of the first `n` even numbers is always `n(n + 1)`.
- The Euclidean Algorithm efficiently computes the greatest common divisor by repeatedly replacing the larger number with the remainder until no remainder exists.

Time Complexity: O(log(min(sumOdd, sumEven)))
- The Euclidean Algorithm runs in logarithmic time.

Space Complexity: O(1)
- Only a few integer variables are used.
